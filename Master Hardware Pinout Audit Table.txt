--Critical Pre-Wiring Checks & Potential Pitfalls
1. ESP32-CAM network access

-Both boards join the same local Wi-Fi network. The CAM Serial Monitor prints its IP address; set CAMERA_SNAPSHOT_URL to http://CAM_IP/capture in Buddy_Robot/secrets.h.

-Do not expose the camera HTTP server to the public internet. It has no authentication and is intended only for a trusted local network.


2. Ultrasonic Echo Voltage Level

-The Issue: The HC-SR04 output on its Echo pin is 5V logic, but ESP32 GPIO pins are not 5V tolerant. GPIO 12 is also a boot-strapping pin on the classic ESP32.

-The Fix: Connect Echo through a divider to GPIO 35 (e.g., 1.8 kOhm from Echo to GPIO 35 and 3.3 kOhm from GPIO 35 to GND), or use a level-shifted 3.3V-compatible sensor.


3. Common Ground Requirement

-The Issue: If the L298N motor driver, main ESP32, ESP32-CAM, and battery pack do not share a ground connection, serial communication, sensor readings, and motor switching will produce erratic glitches or noise.

-The Fix: Ensure a single heavy ground wire ties the GND of all components together.

4. Firmware Coverage

-The Buddy_Robot sketch reads the HC-SR04, fetches camera snapshots over local Wi-Fi, and sends the image and distance to Gemini. The CAM only serves snapshots. The L298N motors are wired but are not controlled by firmware.

5. Credentials and interaction

-Set Wi-Fi credentials in CAM/secrets.h and Buddy_Robot/secrets.h. Set the camera URL and Gemini API key in Buddy_Robot/secrets.h. Do not publish those files or share the API key.

-To chat, open the main ESP32 Serial Monitor at 115200 baud, select a newline line ending, and type a message. Replies appear on the TFT and Serial Monitor. The buzzer can make tones but does not speak; a microphone/audio output is not included.
